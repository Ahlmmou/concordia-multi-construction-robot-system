# Automatic right of way in flexible formation

The motor gate predicts differential-drive trajectories for two seconds using each robot's oriented convex footprint and the configured minimum separation. It checks local obstacle footprints before resolving peer conflicts, then rechecks the final motor commands against stationary peers.

Auxiliaries have priority over tb1. Auxiliary conflicts use stable ascending robot names as the tie breaker. On a conflicting trajectory, the lower priority robot stops both translation and rotation; the other robot continues only if its trajectory is safe against that stopped robot. Robots outside the conflict keep their commands. Normal commands resume automatically once they are safe.

If stopping one robot does not provide a safe route, the flexible formation supervisor selects a temporary clearance waypoint for the priority auxiliary after `yield_recovery_delay` seconds of simulation time (default 3). Lack of actual translational progress also triggers this check, including a robot repeatedly turning in place beside a peer. Candidate waypoints must improve peer clearance, pass the shared map and global costmap checks, and have a sampled straight escape corridor clear of other robots. Nav2 plans and controls the maneuver; the motor gate still enforces local obstacle and swept peer checks. The peer holds until the auxiliary reaches the temporary waypoint, then the formation target resumes. Targets remain stable throughout the maneuver.

Intentional yielding does not count toward the supervisor's progress timeout or exhaust retries if Nav2 aborts the held goal. Operator stops, stale data, localization errors, real obstacles and unresolvable geometry retain their safety stops. Right of way cannot guarantee continuous movement where no collision-free route exists.

The live updates window displays `yielding_to` and `clearance_maneuver` from `/formation/state` and distinguishes yielding from route clearing.

Reverse motion remains available to every robot. Nav2 may choose a negative linear velocity to approach a goal, adjust a corner, or clear a peer without turning in place. The controller preserves its sign, predicts the backward swept footprint (including reverse arcs), and applies the same obstacle clearance and priority rules. The live panel labels backward motion as Reversing. Reverse motion is permitted, not forced; it does not bypass collision checks.

Primary encounters use `primary_minimum_separation: 0.6`: a 0.6 m footprint-to-footprint passing buffer around tb1. Auxiliary pairs retain 0.4 m. The extra primary clearance applies to target reservations, swept movement checks, and clearance waypoint selection, including reversing and turning, without changing the rectangle dimensions. When simulation localization is verified and Gazebo poses are fresh, peer motion checks use physical poses to avoid underestimating clearance due to TF lag. If a robot is already inside the requested buffer, only motion that increases separation without physical overlap is allowed to escape it.

## Leader rolling turns

With `leader_rolling_turn: true`, near-stationary steering commands during flexible travel can become a slow arc at 0.22 m/s and at most 0.15 rad/s (minimum nominal radius about 1.47 m). Forward is preferred unless Nav2 explicitly asks for reverse; the opposite direction is considered only after checking it. The entire two-second arc must clear the shared map, global and local costmaps, and every peer's predicted trajectory with the primary clearance buffer. The normal motor gates then recheck the command. If neither arc fits, the original Nav2 command remains subject to those gates. This assistance never bypasses yielding or operator stops, and does not activate within 1.0 m of the destination, so precise final positioning remains under Nav2. Normal driving commands are preserved. Live status identifies rolling or reversing turns.

## Local obstacle recovery

A local costmap hold lasting three simulation seconds starts recovery for only the affected robot. Its Nav2 action is cancelled and ownership is released before a direct escape begins. Slow forward, reverse and curved candidates are checked over the next two seconds. In an inflated buffer, the padded footprint's overlap must decrease without deepening the zone (one percent raster tolerance); the physical footprint plus cell-extent allowance must remain clear of lethal and unknown cells in the local, global and shared maps. Peer swept checks retain the 0.6 m primary and 0.4 m auxiliary gaps. After leaving the buffer the robot gains at least 0.3 m before resuming, reducing immediate re-entry.

If its current padded footprint is already clear but its requested trajectory is blocked, recovery seeks a verified aligned forward/backward temporary waypoint, then hands movement to Nav2. Formation goals resume after arrival. Robots without a recovery continue normally; operator stops, stale data and localization checks still stop motor commands. If no route is verified, only the recovering robot waits for a safe escape instead of forcing motion through an obstacle. The panel reports escape, detour and waiting states separately.

Auxiliary commands approaching an obstacle are first checked at lower linear speeds while retaining steering, which creates a tighter arc. Pure-turn commands may be reduced in angular speed. Every candidate retains the complete padded-footprint obstacle check and subsequent swept peer checks; speeds are reduced rather than increasing clearance thresholds. Only when none of these candidates fit does the motor gate hold the robot and start recovery. Aligned temporary detours are at least 0.9 m long so the navigation arrival tolerance cannot cancel recovery before the robot gains room to turn.

Reverse is preferred for obstacle escape. A straight reverse corridor with unchanged padded overlap is allowed to continue along an obstacle buffer until its end, provided every swept physical footprint remains clear of lethal/unknown cells, no peer clearance is violated, and padded overlap does not deepen (0.1 percent raster tolerance). Recovery does not finish merely because the first short reverse step succeeds: it requires at least 0.3 m travel and a clear 0.20 m padded footprint before releasing the robot. The old navigation action is cancelled, the saved target is cleared from its action slot, and the next send requests a fresh ComputePathToPose result from the new position.

Target and reservation checks now use oriented footprint clearance, matching the movement gate, instead of circumscribed circles that can reject an otherwise safe final approach. The 0.6 m primary and 0.4 m auxiliary margins are preserved.
